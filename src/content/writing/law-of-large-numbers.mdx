Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots$ be independent, identically distributed random variables with finite expectation $\mu$. The sample mean is

$$\bar X_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i.$$

The weak law says that for every $\varepsilon > 0$,

$$\Pr(|\bar X_n - \mu| > \varepsilon) \to 0.$$

## A statement about probability

This does not say every sequence behaves nicely, nor does it provide a universal sample size. It says that deviation becomes improbable. How quickly depends on the distribution.

```ts
const mean = (xs: number[]) =>
  xs.reduce((total, x) => total + x, 0) / xs.length;
```

For applied work, the asymptotic promise is only the beginning. Concentration inequalities and simulation tell us what “large” might mean in the problem at hand.
